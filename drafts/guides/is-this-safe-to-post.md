Taking your name off a post doesn't remove you from the story. Your cousin knows that wedding, your
coworkers know that meeting, and a town only has so many stories like yours. Here is a quick check
to run before you press post.

[YOU: Have you ever been recognised from something you thought was anonymous, or seen it happen to
someone else? Keep the details vague.]

## 1. Could someone who knows you work out it's you?

Read it as your sister would, or your manager. Specific details do most of the identifying: a job
title, a date, a neighbourhood, or an unusual event. Change or remove them. "My manager" is safer
than "the regional head at my bank", and "a family wedding" is safer than the month and city.

## 2. Could someone work out who it's about?

Even if you stay hidden, the other person might not. It isn't only about being polite. Naming or
clearly pointing to a real person isn't allowed on BajiHears, because that person isn't there to
respond. See the [community guidelines](/guidelines).

## 3. Would it cause harm if it was traced back to you?

Things can go badly if your family, employer or community connects a post to you, and in some
places posts about certain subjects can bring legal or physical risk. [YOU: What should people in
Pakistan keep in mind? Don't give legal advice, but be honest about the risks you're aware of.]

If the answer is yes, keep it private. An [unsent letter](/guides/how-to-write-an-unsent-letter)
gives you most of the relief without the risk.

## 4. Do you understand what "anonymous" covers here?

On BajiHears, other readers can't see who you are unless you choose to post with a handle. But the
service still stores a device identifier for rate limits and moderation, and posts can be read and
copied by anyone. Our [privacy policy](/privacy) has the details.

## 5. Are you okay if it stays up, or gets copied?

Anyone can screenshot a public post, so deleting it may not fully undo it. Give yourself a minute,
and if you still want to say it, say it.

## If you're posting about something serious

If this is about abuse, a crime, or your safety, a public wall isn't the place that can help you.
A trusted person, a local service, or a helpline can actually act on it. Our [help page](/help) is
a good place to start.
