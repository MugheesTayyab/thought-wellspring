An unsent letter is exactly what it sounds like. You write to someone as if they will read it,
knowing they won't. There's no reply to brace for, nobody to protect, and no need to be fair.

## Pick one person and one thing

Most letters stall because they try to cover everything. "Everything I've ever felt about my
father" is a book. "The day you didn't come to my graduation" is a letter. Choose a single moment,
and if more comes up, let it.

## Write it badly on purpose

Start with their name and the first sentence that comes to mind. Some openings that help:

- "What I never told you is..."
- "I still think about the day..."
- "I was angry when you..."
- "I wish you had known..."

Don't fix the spelling or read it back as you go. If you catch yourself writing what you *should*
feel, write that down too: "I'm supposed to have forgiven you by now, and I haven't."

## Give yourself an ending

Set a limit before you begin, such as 15 minutes or one page. A limit makes it easier to start,
and it stops you going round in circles. When you reach it, finish the sentence and stop, even if
it feels unfinished.

## Decide what happens to it

The letter is yours, so there's no correct ending. People usually do one of these:

1. **Keep it** somewhere private, and read it again in a year.
2. **Delete or destroy it**, if the act of getting rid of it feels like the point.
3. **Rewrite it** weeks later, and notice what changed.
4. **Send a real message** that is much shorter and calmer than the letter. Wait at least a few
   days before doing this. The letter was for you, and this message is for them.
5. **Share one line anonymously**, without names or details anyone could recognise. The
   [Wall](/) has a 280-character limit, which helps you find the sentence that matters most.

## When a letter isn't enough

Writing can bring up more than you expected, especially about grief or abuse. Stopping is always
fine. If it leaves you feeling worse for more than a little while, or if you're having thoughts of
harming yourself, please talk to someone. Our [help page](/help) lists free services.

## Keep names out of anything public

An unsent letter can name anyone, as long as it stays private. Once words go online, the rules
change. Before posting, read our short guide on what's safe to share and the
[community guidelines](/guidelines).
